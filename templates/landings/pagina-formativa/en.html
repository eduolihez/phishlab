What to do next time

  1. Stop before you click. Haste is the attacker's main tool. If a message is pressuring you, that alone is reason to be suspicious.
  2. Check the full sender address. Not the display name, the whole address. Domains that look like {{dominio}} are the most common trick.
  3. Never type your password into a page you reached from a link. Use your own bookmark, or type the address yourself.
  4. Report it. Reporting is the most valuable thing you can do: an early warning protects everyone.

What if you entered your password? It is fine, this was a simulation and those credentials were not used. Even so, take the chance to change it, and if you used it anywhere else, change it there too. That habit is what will save you the day the email is real.
